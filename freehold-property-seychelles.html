Freehold Property in Seychelles: A Rare Opportunity

Empathia Village · Investment · June 2026 · 6 min read

A non-Seychellois may acquire privately owned immovable property in Seychelles, including a freehold interest, subject to Government Sanction and the applicable transaction process. The selected title, project classification, conditions, costs and approvals should be checked by an independent Seychelles lawyer before funds are committed.

What freehold actually means

Freehold is the strongest form of property ownership: you own the villa and the land it stands on, outright and without time limit. The title is registered in the Seychelles land registry under a legal system rooted in English and French law, and it is:

  • Transferable — you may sell the property freely;
  • Inheritable — it passes to your heirs like any other owned asset;
  • Mortgageable — it can serve as security for financing.

How the alternatives compare

Most island markets popular with international buyers offer something weaker. In the Maldives, foreign ownership is typically structured as long-term leasehold. In Bali and much of Indonesia, foreigners cannot hold freehold land title and rely on leasehold or nominee arrangements. In Thailand, foreign buyers cannot own land directly and are limited to condominium quotas or 30-year leases. In each case, the "ownership" has an expiry date or a legal workaround at its core.

Freehold in Seychelles can offer ownership without a fixed lease expiry, but acquisition by a non-Seychellois remains subject to Government Sanction, transaction costs and the conditions applicable to the specific property.

Scarcity is structural here: Seychelles' total population is barely 100,000, buildable land on Mahé is limited by the granite terrain, and Empathia Village itself is intentionally limited to 30 plots.

What this means for value

Clean title is the foundation of long-term value. A freehold villa in a supply-constrained market, neighbouring the Four Seasons and Kempinski resorts above Baie Lazare, combines scarcity and lifestyle appeal. Any rental use depends on the intended model and may require licensing, management and tax compliance. Occupancy and returns are not guaranteed; confirm the permitted use with qualified Seychelles advisers.
